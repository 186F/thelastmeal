# 1. Executive statement

**Research objective.** The project seeks a general architecture for simulated
lives in which a deterministic engine maintains persistent, consequential, and
causally authoritative reality, while generative models perform bounded forms
of interpretation, reflection, imagination, social reasoning, planning,
language, and psychological development. The research question is how those
forms of cognition should be allocated, combined, and scheduled, not whether
either deterministic or generative control should replace the other wholesale.

The intended target is a population of believable, vibrant lives that persist,
remain causally grounded, can surprise and develop histories, and are compelling
to observe while resisting repetition, nonsense, drift, and dead-end
interaction.

This charter frames a research program. It does not authorize implementation or
experimentation, and it does not claim that the intended target has been
achieved.

# 2. Revised problem statement

> Which cognitive functions should be deterministic, which should be
> generative, and at what timescales should each operate so that long-running
> simulated lives remain persistent, grounded, varied, causally intelligible,
> and compelling to observe?

**Research objective.** The primary objective is to learn how the allocation
and timing of cognitive authority affect long-running simulated lives. The
project is not primarily trying to:

- minimize API cost for its own sake;
- maximize model calls;
- replace all game logic with a language model;
- add decorative dialogue to deterministic utility agents;
- create one impressive scripted demonstration; or
- optimize only task completion, persona consistency, or numerical similarity.

Calls, tokens, latency, and cost remain useful operational diagnostics. They do
not, by themselves, determine whether a simulated life meets the research
objective.

## Hypothesized risk: excessive generative authority

Excessive generative authority may produce:

- repetition and semantic drift;
- hallucinated facts or forgotten causality;
- mutual echoing and conversations that do not terminate;
- goal churn or repeated sterile activity;
- rationales that stand in for persistent motives; and
- narrative overinterpretation in which every event becomes equally
  significant.

## Hypothesized risk: excessive deterministic control

Excessive deterministic control may produce:

- predictable utility maximization;
- trait-weighted automata or caricatures;
- little interpretation, ambivalence, invention, or surprise;
- the same motive repeatedly producing the same action; and
- dialogue that merely decorates behavior rather than participating in a
  persistent life.

These are opposed hypotheses and risks to test, not inevitable outcomes or
universal conclusions about either form of control.

# 3. Reinterpretation of sparse cognition

> **Milestone 3 definition:** Sparse cognition means bounded generative
> authority over particular cognitive functions, not necessarily a small
> absolute number of model calls.

Call frequency describes how often a model is invoked. Cognitive authority
describes what the model is permitted to interpret, propose, or determine.
These are separate variables:

- A system could make many bounded reflection, interpretation, or language
  calls without giving the model authority over objective world truth.
- A system could make few calls yet grant excessive authority if those calls
  can rewrite state, history, or identity.
- Model-call reduction remains an operational diagnostic and a possible
  consequence of a successful allocation, not the milestone's primary
  objective.

This definition applies prospectively to Milestone 3. It does not retroactively
change Milestone 2's registered objective, experiment, findings, or claim
boundaries, and it does not select a candidate cognitive architecture.

# 4. What Milestone 2 taught us

The [Milestone 2 closeout report](../002-sparse-cognition/MILESTONE_002_CLOSEOUT_REPORT.md)
provides the interpretation and final claim boundary. The
[canonical production analysis](../002-sparse-cognition/calibration/m2-calibration-variance-a-001.analysis.md)
provides the registered calibration results. The statements below are
historical findings bounded to that exact study and condition.

## Historical finding: systems

The deterministic laboratory, unattended evidence path, strict finalization,
replay, registration binding, and canonical analysis worked together to produce
a valid ten-run calibration. Ten valid primary runs completed with high
accepted-model coverage and consistent model routing under the tested
condition; every run was strict-finalized and replay-verified.

## Historical finding: behavior

The tested per-decision condition was highly repeatable under the registered
metric. Meaningful behavioral breadth was nevertheless limited: observed
model-choice variation was concentrated in a consequence-free
rest-versus-routine-work branch after the purifier task was complete, and the
major consequential outcomes were the same across all ten runs.

Canonical state represented Mara as the creditor entitled to relief and Jonas
as the debtor obligated to provide it. The model's diagnostic outputs
repeatedly misframed Mara's continued bench work as an obligation to Jonas.
The selected actions remained legal, engine-accepted, and task-effective, so
the recorded defect is semantic rather than mechanical. The record does not
establish the model's internal reason for this framing, and the closeout also
notes that the engine-supplied affordance wording was an inversion-suggestive
surface.

## Historical finding: methodological lesson

Operational reliability and high behavioral similarity can coexist with
behavior that is narrow, semantically mistaken, or unworthy of preservation as
a believable-life target. Task success, similarity, identity expression,
semantic correctness, and behavioral breadth are distinct properties; success
on task completion and registered similarity does not establish the others.

## What Milestone 2 did not establish

- It did not prove that every per-decision architecture fails.
- It did not prove that policy patches or deterministic control are superior.
- It did not establish human behavioral realism.
- It did not resolve the scalable AI-NPC problem.
- The policy condition did not fail; it was not implemented, registered,
  measured, or run.

# 5. North-star evaluation target

**Future evaluation properties.** The following properties define what future
Milestone 3 work should seek to evaluate. They are research targets, not claims
about the current system, and this charter does not freeze their formulas,
thresholds, or study design.

## Persistence

Experiences leave durable, traceable effects on memory, belief, relationships,
commitments, goals, and later behavior.

## Causal grounding

NPCs act from information they could possess. Interpretation cannot rewrite
objective history or bypass world constraints.

## Coherent individuality

A character remains recognizable over time without becoming a rigid
caricature.

## Behavioral breadth

A character can produce more than one defensible trajectory when circumstances
permit genuine discretion.

## Adaptation

New evidence and consequences can revise beliefs, strategies, relationships,
and conduct.

## Emergence

Small differences can produce larger life differences whose causal path remains
intelligible.

## Non-degeneracy

The system resists action loops, dialogue repetition, mutual echoing, sterile
goal churn, generic optimization, arbitrary drift, and conversations that
neither produce consequences nor end.

## Observer value

Lives are sufficiently legible, surprising, and consequential that an observer
wants to continue watching or investigating them. Observer value is a
legitimate product and research objective, but future measurement must be
specified and pre-registered rather than asserted from anecdotes.

# 15. Explicit exclusions

**Explicit exclusion.** Completing or approving this charter does not, by
itself:

- authorize implementation, a new prompt, condition, schema, experiment, model
  call, or live study;
- select the episode-appraisal/policy-artifact design, or any other
  candidate, as the accepted architecture;
- authorize a parallel laboratory, headless runner, inference broker, cloud
  deployment, or concurrency change;
- authorize comparator or scenario remediation;
- authorize the Truman-style, Apartment Building, or additional colony
  scenarios;
- alter Milestone 2 evidence, metrics, analysis, findings, or claim boundaries;
- establish human behavioral realism;
- support a claim of novelty or priority, architectural superiority, or
  resolution of the broad AI-NPC problem; or
- make cost or model-call reduction the sole measure of success.

These exclusions also prevent the charter from silently authorizing a later
work package. Each such package requires a separate, explicit gate.
