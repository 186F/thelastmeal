# Milestone 3 Research Charter — Parallel Causal Life Laboratory

**Document status:** WORKING DRAFT under Milestone 3 Item 2.

**Authorization:** This document does not authorize implementation or
experimentation.

**Completion:** Each section remains incomplete until its assigned chunk is
approved.

# 1. Executive statement

**Research objective.** The project seeks a general architecture for simulated
lives in which a deterministic engine maintains persistent, consequential, and
causally authoritative reality, while generative models perform bounded forms
of interpretation, reflection, imagination, social reasoning, planning,
language, and psychological development. The research question is how those
forms of cognition should be allocated, combined, and scheduled, not whether
either deterministic or generative control should replace the other wholesale.

The intended target is a population of believable, vibrant lives that persist,
remain causally grounded, can surprise and develop histories, and are compelling
to observe while resisting repetition, nonsense, drift, and dead-end
interaction.

This charter frames a research program. It does not authorize implementation or
experimentation, and it does not claim that the intended target has been
achieved.

# 2. Revised problem statement

> Which cognitive functions should be deterministic, which should be
> generative, and at what timescales should each operate so that long-running
> simulated lives remain persistent, grounded, varied, causally intelligible,
> and compelling to observe?

**Research objective.** The primary objective is to learn how the allocation
and timing of cognitive authority affect long-running simulated lives. The
project is not primarily trying to:

- minimize API cost for its own sake;
- maximize model calls;
- replace all game logic with a language model;
- add decorative dialogue to deterministic utility agents;
- create one impressive scripted demonstration; or
- optimize only task completion, persona consistency, or numerical similarity.

Calls, tokens, latency, and cost remain useful operational diagnostics. They do
not, by themselves, determine whether a simulated life meets the research
objective.

## Hypothesized risk: excessive generative authority

Excessive generative authority may produce:

- repetition and semantic drift;
- hallucinated facts or forgotten causality;
- mutual echoing and conversations that do not terminate;
- goal churn or repeated sterile activity;
- rationales that stand in for persistent motives; and
- narrative overinterpretation in which every event becomes equally
  significant.

## Hypothesized risk: excessive deterministic control

Excessive deterministic control may produce:

- predictable utility maximization;
- trait-weighted automata or caricatures;
- little interpretation, ambivalence, invention, or surprise;
- the same motive repeatedly producing the same action; and
- dialogue that merely decorates behavior rather than participating in a
  persistent life.

These are opposed hypotheses and risks to test, not inevitable outcomes or
universal conclusions about either form of control.

# 3. Reinterpretation of sparse cognition

> **Milestone 3 definition:** Sparse cognition means bounded generative
> authority over particular cognitive functions, not necessarily a small
> absolute number of model calls.

Call frequency describes how often a model is invoked. Cognitive authority
describes what the model is permitted to interpret, propose, or determine.
These are separate variables:

- A system could make many bounded reflection, interpretation, or language
  calls without giving the model authority over objective world truth.
- A system could make few calls yet grant excessive authority if those calls
  can rewrite state, history, or identity.
- Model-call reduction remains an operational diagnostic and a possible
  consequence of a successful allocation, not the milestone's primary
  objective.

This definition applies prospectively to Milestone 3. It does not retroactively
change Milestone 2's registered objective, experiment, findings, or claim
boundaries, and it does not select a candidate cognitive architecture.

# 4. What Milestone 2 taught us

The [Milestone 2 closeout report](../002-sparse-cognition/MILESTONE_002_CLOSEOUT_REPORT.md)
provides the interpretation and final claim boundary. The
[canonical production analysis](../002-sparse-cognition/calibration/m2-calibration-variance-a-001.analysis.md)
provides the registered calibration results. The statements below are
historical findings bounded to that exact study and condition.

## Historical finding: systems

The deterministic laboratory, unattended evidence path, strict finalization,
replay, registration binding, and canonical analysis worked together to produce
a valid ten-run calibration. Ten valid primary runs completed with high
accepted-model coverage and consistent model routing under the tested
condition; every run was strict-finalized and replay-verified.

## Historical finding: behavior

The tested per-decision condition was highly repeatable under the registered
metric. Meaningful behavioral breadth was nevertheless limited: observed
model-choice variation was concentrated in a consequence-free
rest-versus-routine-work branch after the purifier task was complete, and the
major consequential outcomes were the same across all ten runs.

Canonical state represented Mara as the creditor entitled to relief and Jonas
as the debtor obligated to provide it. The model's diagnostic outputs
repeatedly misframed Mara's continued bench work as an obligation to Jonas.
The selected actions remained legal, engine-accepted, and task-effective, so
the recorded defect is semantic rather than mechanical. The record does not
establish the model's internal reason for this framing, and the closeout also
notes that the engine-supplied affordance wording was an inversion-suggestive
surface.

## Historical finding: methodological lesson

Operational reliability and high behavioral similarity can coexist with
behavior that is narrow, semantically mistaken, or unworthy of preservation as
a believable-life target. Task success, similarity, identity expression,
semantic correctness, and behavioral breadth are distinct properties; success
on task completion and registered similarity does not establish the others.

## What Milestone 2 did not establish

- It did not prove that every per-decision architecture fails.
- It did not prove that policy patches or deterministic control are superior.
- It did not establish human behavioral realism.
- It did not resolve the scalable AI-NPC problem.
- The policy condition did not fail; it was not implemented, registered,
  measured, or run.

# 5. North-star evaluation target

**Future evaluation properties.** The following properties define what future
Milestone 3 work should seek to evaluate. They are research targets, not claims
about the current system, and this charter does not freeze their formulas,
thresholds, or study design.

## Persistence

Experiences leave durable, traceable effects on memory, belief, relationships,
commitments, goals, and later behavior.

## Causal grounding

NPCs act from information they could possess. Interpretation cannot rewrite
objective history or bypass world constraints.

## Coherent individuality

A character remains recognizable over time without becoming a rigid
caricature.

## Behavioral breadth

A character can produce more than one defensible trajectory when circumstances
permit genuine discretion.

## Adaptation

New evidence and consequences can revise beliefs, strategies, relationships,
and conduct.

## Emergence

Small differences can produce larger life differences whose causal path remains
intelligible.

## Non-degeneracy

The system resists action loops, dialogue repetition, mutual echoing, sterile
goal churn, generic optimization, arbitrary drift, and conversations that
neither produce consequences nor end.

## Observer value

Lives are sufficiently legible, surprising, and consequential that an observer
wants to continue watching or investigating them. Observer value is a
legitimate product and research objective, but future measurement must be
specified and pre-registered rather than asserted from anecdotes.

# 6. Scenario families

> These are provisional research laboratories for testing one underlying
> simulated-life architecture. They are not authorized implementations,
> product commitments, or claims that the architecture can already support
> them.

Each family is a candidate future evaluation context, not a selected scenario
or a scenario-construction plan. Building or studying any family would require
separate authorization.

## 6.1 Colony-pressure laboratory

**Provisional research focus.** This RimWorld-inspired systemic environment
would place simulated lives amid:

- scarcity and resource pressure;
- work, competence, and practical responsibility;
- emergencies and competing duties;
- commitments and their consequences;
- group formation;
- resentment, gratitude, trust, and conflict; and
- shared-world events that create later social history.

> **Primary question:** Can distinct people create an emergent social history
> inside a demanding systemic world?

This family would test behavior under systemic pressure. Task efficiency may
be an observable outcome, but it is neither the preferred outcome nor a
sufficient measure of the social history produced under that pressure.

## 6.2 Developmental epistemology laboratory

**Provisional research focus.** The Truman-style family would provide a
longitudinal test of development, belief formation, and world-model revision,
including:

- early development;
- concept acquisition;
- attention and curiosity;
- social trust;
- theory formation;
- hypothesis testing;
- false explanations;
- belief revision; and
- development of a model of self and world.

An NPC merely saying "this is a simulation" is not success. A meaningful
discovery would require causally grounded observations, accumulated evidence,
competing explanations, hypothesis testing, and belief revision. Any future
study would have to prevent premise leakage: the model must not simply be told
the hidden answer.

This family does not specify or authorize a child-development implementation,
age model, prompt, sample size, or success threshold.

## 6.3 Ambient-community laboratory

**Provisional research focus.** The Apartment Building family would test lives
that continue without centering the observer, including:

- routines and employment;
- friendships, romance, and household change;
- financial and practical pressures;
- visitors, secrets, conflict, arrivals, and departures;
- quiet periods;
- offscreen continuity; and
- longitudinal relationship change.

> **Primary question:** Can residents appear to have lives that existed before
> observation and continue when the user looks away?

Inventing a retrospective biography when an observer arrives would not satisfy
this question. Future work would have to evaluate persistent causal continuity:
events, relationships, commitments, and changes must endure and shape later
conduct, including while residents are offscreen.

# 7. Candidate allocations of cognitive authority

> These are candidate conditions and controls. The charter does not presume
> which allocation will perform best, and complexity must earn its place
> experimentally.

Every condition below is provisional future work. The descriptions establish a
neutral comparison space; they do not select an architecture or authorize an
implementation, experiment, prompt, schema, model call, or runtime mechanism.

## 7.1 Deterministic control

**Model authority.** None. Generative cognition neither selects actions nor
maintains cognitive state.

**Engine authority.** The deterministic engine selects actions, maintains
cognitive and world state, and owns legality and consequences.

**Research question tested.** What social and behavioral emergence already
exists in deterministic world dynamics, and what incremental value, if any,
appears when bounded generative cognition is introduced?

**Principal risk or limitation.** This condition cannot expose the effects of
generative interpretation because it excludes that interpretation by design.
It is nevertheless a mechanical control, not a condition presumed to be
lifeless or inferior.

## 7.2 Per-decision generative control

**Model authority.** The model selects every high-level action from
engine-legal affordances.

**Engine authority.** The engine defines the legal affordances and remains
authoritative over objective state, legality, fallback behavior, and
consequences.

**Research question tested.** As a direct comparator, what behavioral breadth
and semantic comprehension appear, and what repetition, drift,
latency/fallback effects, and long-horizon consequences arise?

**Principal risk or limitation.** Repeated generative selection may repeat,
drift, misunderstand context, or expose execution to latency and fallback
effects. Milestone 2's result remains bounded to its exact tested condition and
does not establish that all per-decision designs share those limitations.

## 7.3 Episode-appraisal / policy-artifact cognition

**Model authority.** The model interprets bounded motivational episodes and may
produce an episode-scoped motivational artifact, including partial coverage or
abstention. It does not select every action.

**Engine authority.** The engine owns psychological primitives, objective world
truth, legal action selection, and consequences. It uses the artifact as one
influence when selecting among legal actions.

**Research question tested.** Can bounded episodic interpretation create
persistent, causally intelligible motivational influence without per-decision
model control?

**Principal risk or limitation.** The candidate may overinterpret events,
encode semantic errors, or add complexity without behavioral value.

Its candidate conceptual features are limited here to:

- quiet-event candidates;
- bounded reflection windows;
- episode-scoped motivational artifacts;
- engine-owned psychological primitives;
- bounded acyclic motivational graphs;
- multiple simultaneous motives;
- explicit motivational conflict;
- partial coverage and abstention;
- immutable successor lineages; and
- persistent memory, belief, relationship, and emotional residue after episode
  closure.

This is a candidate, not the accepted architecture. The full conceptual design
has not been implemented or experimentally validated. This charter authorizes
no schema, constant, operator vocabulary, prompt, or runtime mechanism for it.

## 7.4 Reflection-only cognition

**Model authority.** The model may review bounded experience and propose
bounded memory, belief, relationship, or self-narrative consolidation subject
to future engine validation.

**Engine authority.** The deterministic engine selects actions, preserves
objective history, and would validate any proposed consolidation. Reflection
output cannot rewrite objective history.

**Research question tested.** Can richer long-term lives emerge without
generative action selection?

**Principal risk or limitation.** Reflection may invent, self-reinforce, or
distort narratives, or it may fail to affect later conduct.

## 7.5 Planning-only cognition

**Model authority.** The model forms bounded higher-level intentions or plans.

**Engine authority.** Deterministic systems execute, interrupt, and revise
plans against authoritative world state and legal action constraints.

**Research question tested.** Does generative long-horizon direction add value
without per-action model control?

**Principal risk or limitation.** Plans may become stale, brittle, disconnected
from execution, or sources of goal churn. This candidate defines no plan schema
or caching implementation.

## 7.6 Dialogue-only generation over engine-selected intent

**Model authority.** The model verbalizes an engine-provided social intent
within permitted content boundaries.

**Engine authority.** The engine selects social intent and permissible content
and owns authoritative consequences.

**Research question tested.** Can conversational expression improve while
behavioral and causal authority remain engine-owned, separating dialogue
quality from behavioral authority?

**Principal risk or limitation.** Fluent language may merely decorate shallow
intent or stray beyond its bounds. Engine-selected intent is not presumed
sufficient for believable social life.

## 7.7 Mixed multi-timescale cognition

**Model authority.** The model participates in bounded appraisal, reflection,
consolidation, relationship or identity development, and developmental
world-model change at different timescales.

**Engine authority.** The engine owns objective state, legality, and
consequences throughout, including deterministic reflexes, needs, movement,
and physical action at short timescales.

**Research question tested.** Which timescale-specific layers, if any, add
value beyond simpler allocations, and does a combined allocation retain that
value under ablation and comparison?

**Principal risk or limitation.** This is the broadest and most complex
candidate. Cross-layer inconsistency and experimental confounding are risks;
every layer must earn its place through ablation and comparison rather than an
assumption that more modules create better lives.

The conceptual candidate division is:

- **Seconds/minutes:** deterministic reflexes, needs, movement, and physical
  action.
- **Minutes/hours:** bounded episode appraisal.
- **Hours/days:** reflection and consolidation.
- **Weeks/years:** relationship and identity development.
- **Months/lifetime:** developmental world-model change.

These timescale bands describe candidate authority layers, not an implementation
decomposition.

# 8. Potentially distinctive research contribution

The broad aspiration of believable autonomous characters and emergent
simulated societies is not novel.
[CiF](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIIDE/article/view/12454) and
[Versu](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/6648395) predate LLM agents and
already demonstrate authored social state, reusable social rules or practices,
engine-owned consequences, and autonomous character choice beneath
authoritative state; Versu additionally demonstrates deterministic,
serializable simulation with exact replay and inspection. The candidate
contribution is the combination and experimental treatment of the five
elements below, not the invention of their components.

> **Governing claim boundary:** This combination is a candidate contribution
> requiring continued literature review and empirical validation.

## 8.1 Adaptive cognitive-authority allocation

**Established predecessor elements.** CiF and Versu separate authored or
engine-owned social consequences from player or autonomous-character choice;
Versu further separates decision, coordination, continuity, and drama
management. BDI commitment policies govern persistence, reconsideration, and
goal termination under resource bounds. Mixed-initiative work routes among
inaction, clarification, and autonomous action using uncertainty and utility.
Recent systems also provide configurable or fixed agent, controller, policy,
and environment roles. [CiF](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIIDE/article/view/12454),
[Versu](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/6648395),
[BDI](https://aaai.org/papers/icmas95-042-bdi-agents-from-theory-to-practice/),
[mixed initiative](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/principles-mixed-initiative-user-interfaces/),
[Concordia](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.03664),
[Project Sid](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00114),
[AgentSociety](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08691), and
[PCSP](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23652) establish these component-level
precedents.

**What the charter proposes to combine or test differently.** A future study
could register a runtime policy that allocates a bounded cognitive transition
among a model, deterministic engine, reusable policy or artifact, and an
abstention or escalation path according to persistent-life state. The
allocation schedule would itself be a treatment rather than an implementation
detail.

**Bounded contribution hypothesis.** A registered model-versus-engine
cognitive-authority schedule, compared from matched persistent-life ancestors
under the same authoritative mechanics and preregistered outcome definitions,
with branch-specific evidence lineage preserved and observed outcomes allowed
to diverge, was **not located within the bounded corpus reviewed for this
charter**.

**Claim boundary.** The charter does not claim component-level priority,
general benefit, or superiority over a fixed allocation. The hypothesis must
survive broader review and matched empirical comparison.

## 8.2 Counterfactual cognitive twins

**Established predecessor elements.** Versu serializes deterministic social
state and exactly replays selected actions; DPC holds persona and event
sequences fixed across variants; current-agent systems use ablations,
interventions, or trace replay. Common-random-number methods already create
paired simulated individuals across scenarios and support paired inference.
[Versu](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/6648395),
[DPC](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.1336/),
[AI Metropolis](https://proceedings.mlsys.org/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/4f31327e046913c7238d5b671f5d820e-Abstract-Conference.html),
and [Stout and Goldie](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2761656/)
establish those precedents. Event-keyed randomness further shows why a shared
seed can misalign stochastic events after an intervention changes execution
order. [Buffalo, Pearson, and Klein](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.11084)
formalize that risk.

**What the charter proposes to combine or test differently.** A future twin
would serialize the relevant world, memories, relationships, motives,
affordances, evidence references, model and prompt lineage, and stochastic
event identity, then alter a registered authority treatment and compare
replicated long-horizon descendants.

**Bounded contribution hypothesis.** Routine common-ancestor forks that join
that persistent inner and social state to a registered authority intervention,
authoritative evidence ancestry, stable event identity, and longitudinal
paired outcomes were **not located within the bounded corpus reviewed for this
charter**.

**Claim boundary.** A shared initial snapshot or seed is not itself a
contribution and does not by itself establish a coherent causal comparison.
The intervention boundary, event matching, conditional descendant events,
lineage, and replication would all require specification and validation.

## 8.3 Episode-scoped, provenance-bearing inner life

**Established predecessor elements.** Prior systems already persist memories,
reflections, plans, social facts, event/perception links, corrections, skills,
and autonomously managed records. SPASM stores perspective-neutral dialogue
history and deterministically projects agent-relative views; Versu serializes
complete state and supports replay and inspection. These are established
record, reuse, and perspective precedents in
[Generative Agents](https://doi.org/10.1145/3586183.3606763),
[Voyager](https://openreview.net/forum?id=ehfRiF0R3a),
[AgentSociety](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08691),
[Agentopia](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.07513),
[DPC](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.1336/),
[CiF](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIIDE/article/view/12454),
[Versu](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/6648395), and
[SPASM](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.412/).

**What the charter proposes to combine or test differently.** A bounded
epistemic episode could record authoritative world evidence, what the agent
could and could not observe, motive conflict, explicit uncertainty or
abstention, the selected authority, the action and engine consequence, and an
immutable ancestor/successor and reuse lineage.

**Bounded contribution hypothesis.** The integration of episode boundaries,
source-level epistemic coverage, engine-grounded consequence, abstention, and
inspectable cross-episode lineage in a persistent social life was **not located
within the bounded corpus reviewed for this charter**.

**Claim boundary.** Existing transcript projection, state serialization,
social history, memory, reflection, and audit metadata remain predecessor
elements. The proposed combination does not establish authentic inner life,
truthful latent psychology, or behavioral improvement.

## 8.4 Direct measurement of cognitive dead ends

**Established predecessor elements.** SPASM measures persona drift and
echoing and implements semantic stopping; DPC measures dynamic coherence and
character collapse; interacting-agent work measures personality consistency
and linguistic alignment. Project Sid reports action loops and saturation,
Voyager reports stuck or incorrectly verified behavior, and PCSP reports
traceability and engine-execution problems. BDI and mixed-initiative systems
also provide explicit termination, timeout, withdrawal, or graceful
degradation. These precedents are documented in
[SPASM](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.412/),
[DPC](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.1336/),
[LLM Agents in Interaction](https://aclanthology.org/2024.personalize-1.9/),
[Project Sid](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00114),
[Voyager](https://openreview.net/forum?id=ehfRiF0R3a),
[PCSP](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23652),
[BDI](https://aaai.org/papers/icmas95-042-bdi-agents-from-theory-to-practice/),
and [mixed initiative](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/principles-mixed-initiative-user-interfaces/).

**What the charter proposes to combine or test differently.** A future
registered suite could join world-state, action, goal, and dialogue repetition;
consequence-free activity; goal churn; incoherent abandonment; identity and
role drift; recovery; social echoing; and independent observer willingness to
continue. Every result would link to the authoritative trajectory that
produced it.

**Bounded contribution hypothesis.** A unified long-horizon suite combining
machine-detectable trajectory dead ends, recovery, and independent observer
evaluation across matched persistent lives was **not located within the
bounded corpus reviewed for this charter**.

**Claim boundary.** Drift, echoing, loops, stuck goals, engine aborts, and
stopping behavior already have predecessor measures or reports. The proposed
suite is neither validated nor complete, and an unmeasured question must not be
reported as an observed defect.

## 8.5 High-throughput causal architecture search

**Established predecessor elements.** AI Metropolis uses dependency-aware
out-of-order execution; AgentSociety uses a distributed runtime; Project Sid
uses concurrent cognitive modules; and PCSP scales a shared persona-conditioned
policy. Common-random-number designs provide paired variance reduction, while
event-keyed randomness decouples stochastic draws from execution order. These
elements are established separately by
[AI Metropolis](https://proceedings.mlsys.org/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/4f31327e046913c7238d5b671f5d820e-Abstract-Conference.html),
[AgentSociety](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08691),
[Project Sid](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00114),
[PCSP](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23652),
[Stout and Goldie](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2761656/), and
[Buffalo, Pearson, and Klein](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.11084).

**What the charter proposes to combine or test differently.** Concurrency
could expand a preregistered search over authority architectures after
serial/parallel behavioral parity, event/noise identity, common ancestry,
evidence lineage, and paired replication are protected.

**Bounded contribution hypothesis.** High-throughput execution used expressly
to expand registered, long-horizon causal comparison of cognitive-authority
architectures under those evidence-integrity constraints was **not located
within the bounded corpus reviewed for this charter**.

**Claim boundary.** Throughput does not establish scientific validity;
parallel execution is not presumed behaviorally neutral; and population,
duration, interaction, and throughput counts from different evaluations are
not interchangeable evidence.

# 9. Relationship to existing research

This map organizes the bounded corpus by research lineage. It distinguishes
paper-reported outcomes and problems from author-stated limitations, questions
not evaluated, and this charter's inferences.

## 9.1 Memory, reflection, planning, and generative societies

Generative Agents combines a memory stream, retrieval, reflection, planning,
and reaction in a 25-agent, two-simulated-day sandbox. Its authors report
information diffusion and coordinated party attendance, and human raters
judged the full architecture more believable than its ablations. Reported
failures include retrieval misses, hallucinated or embellished memories and
relationships, and planning or action-selection errors. Author-stated
limitations include the study's small, short, costly design and the fact that
believability does not establish human or societal validity; long-horizon
stability, large populations, and causal forks were not evaluated.
[Generative Agents](https://doi.org/10.1145/3586183.3606763)

Project Sid/PIANO decomposes cognition into concurrent model and non-model
modules and reports individual, social, economic, and cultural simulations in
Minecraft. Its authors also report hallucination, miscommunication, action
loops, saturation, and infrastructure problems; its analyzed 500-agent
cultural result is a descriptive run rather than replicated evidence for a
1,000-agent interacting society. [Project Sid](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00114)

AgentSociety joins structured agents, social/economic spaces, linked event and
perception records, and distributed infrastructure. Its million-scale result
is an environment-object load test, whereas its 10,000-model-agent systems
test lasts five rounds. Reported systems bottlenecks include slowest-worker
imbalance and external-model latency. The authors identify incomplete
social/economic coverage and limited realism and human calibration as
limitations; the hurricane study separately reports magnitude and speed
mismatches.
[AgentSociety](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08691)

Agentopia reports 300 agents across three worlds for ten simulated years. In
its design, a generative environment model supplies events and consequential
feedback. Reported failures include hallucinated nonexistent people and
places. The authors identify turn-based operation, AI-only feedback, uncertain
environmental and reward validity, and compute-limited coverage as limitations.
The charter therefore treats the result as evidence of simulation duration,
not human or causal validity.
[Agentopia](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.07513)

This project may adopt explicit memory/reflection/planning decompositions,
event-to-perception links, dynamic state, long-horizon accounting, and targeted
ablations. It proposes to test them with an authoritative causal substrate,
registered authority treatments, independent outcome evaluation, and direct
trajectory measures.

## 9.2 Executable worlds, policies, and model/engine authority

Concordia supplies a configurable agent/Game-Master component framework in
which a generative Game Master can interpret actions and simulate effects. Its
paper is an architectural and example-based contribution, not a controlled
behavioral solution. [Concordia](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.03664)

Voyager couples model-generated curricula and executable skill artifacts to
Minecraft feedback and reuses skills recorded after model self-verification.
The authors report stuck behavior, incorrect self-verification, impossible
curriculum items, and invalid items or APIs; the study concerns one embodied
task agent rather than persistent social or psychological life.
[Voyager](https://openreview.net/forum?id=ehfRiF0R3a)

One Policy, Infinite NPCs (PCSP) encodes personas once and conditions a shared
reinforcement-learning policy whose semantic intents execute through game
substrates. Within its tested tasks, the paper reports real-time scaling and
persona traceability. Measured negative results include zero top-1
classification in the vocabulary-expansion held-out test, reduced alignment
and behavior-tree aborts under contention, and chance-level traceability after
the InfoNCE ablation even though task reward was preserved or improved.
Personas remain static, while dynamic memory, open-world longitudinal
individuality, and broader believability were not evaluated.
[PCSP](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23652)

These works justify adopting executable feedback, inspectable reusable
artifacts, explicit agent/environment roles, and traceability separate from
task reward. This charter proposes to compare who may decide a cognitive
transition while the same deterministic substrate retains legality and durable
consequences.

## 9.3 Persona stability, perspective projection, and interaction drift

DPC separates stable identity from short- and mid-term adaptation across
synthetic 100–150-turn event schedules and reports higher persona-coherence
scores plus repair of severe violations. Measured negatives include
cumulative-stress weakness in static baselines and 9.5% drift in one
configuration. Its events, state deltas, responses, and primary judgments are
substantially model-mediated. Author-stated limitations include unexplored
layer decompositions, possible primary-judge bias, and incomplete multilingual
validation; authoritative persistent worlds and independent human-observer
validation were not evaluated.
[DPC](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.1336/)

LLM Agents in Interaction reports asymmetric personality and linguistic
convergence after a one-sided, one-step text interaction between extreme
persona groups. It does not evaluate a persistent world, multi-turn social
history, consequences, or anti-convergence interventions.
[LLM Agents in Interaction](https://aclanthology.org/2024.personalize-1.9/)

SPASM stores perspective-neutral dialogue history and deterministically
projects agent-relative views; in its tested dialogue setting, the authors
report reduced persona drift and zero human-observed ECP echoing under the
stated validation procedure. The paper's prose and Table 4 caption describe
the human-annotation coverage differently, so the exact validation scope
should not be overstated. SPASM also provides semantic stopping, but it does
not evaluate persistent worlds, embodied action, durable social relationships,
or open-ended lives. [SPASM](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.412/)

This project may adopt identity-versus-adaptation separation, matched context
construction, asymmetric convergence analysis, direct drift trajectories,
human echoing labels, and termination records. It proposes to connect those
measures to authoritative world evidence and persistent, multi-episode social
consequences.

## 9.4 Pre-LLM social simulation and autonomous storytelling

CiF represents traits, relationships, statuses, social networks, cultural
knowledge, and social history in an authored symbolic engine. Reusable
microtheories and social exchanges govern volition and engine-owned effects;
the authors demonstrate design and authoring capabilities but report no
controlled behavioral evaluation and identify difficult manual tuning.
[CiF](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIIDE/article/view/12454)

Versu gives autonomous agents utility-based action choice within role-neutral
social practices while a deterministic engine owns serializable state. It
supports exact replay and inspection, but its authors conducted no controlled
experiment and report costly content production, restricted beliefs,
short-horizon choice, and difficult multi-practice tuning.
[Versu](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/6648395)

These systems establish that authored social state, reusable practices,
engine-owned social consequences, autonomous characters, deterministic social
simulation, and exact replay predate the LLM-agent lineage. This project may
adopt inspectable social history, practices, authoritative effects, and replay;
it proposes to treat authority allocation as a registered comparison and to
measure persistent-life outcomes directly.

## 9.5 Deliberation, mixed initiative, and authority allocation

BDI architecture separates beliefs, desires, and intentions and supplies
explicit option generation, commitment, reconsideration, execution, and
success/impossibility termination under resource bounds. Its practical form
sacrifices expressive power, and the cited OASIS account supplies application
architecture and live-data trials without controlled outcome statistics.
[BDI](https://aaai.org/papers/icmas95-042-bdi-agents-from-theory-to-practice/)

Horvitz's mixed-initiative LookOut system selects among inaction,
clarification, and autonomous calendaring using uncertain intent, utility, and
attention, while preserving user invocation, correction, termination, and
final-save authority. The paper presents a working prototype and limited
training/timing evidence, but not a controlled comparison of initiative modes.
[Principles of Mixed-Initiative User Interfaces](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/principles-mixed-initiative-user-interfaces/)

These lineages establish that persistence, reconsideration, deferral,
clarification, action, termination, and graceful degradation are established
control concerns. This charter's bounded question is how heterogeneous
computational authorities should be allocated and tested within persistent
simulated social lives.

## 9.6 Parallel execution and paired causal simulation

AI Metropolis reports dependency-aware out-of-order scheduling speedups for
language-model multi-agent workloads. Its direct end-to-end study uses 25
agents; larger scale-outs concatenate independently traced SmallVilles, so the
1,000-agent point is systems evidence rather than one live interacting society.
Conservative dependencies, lighter workloads, and hardware saturation limit
the reported gains.
[AI Metropolis](https://proceedings.mlsys.org/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/4f31327e046913c7238d5b671f5d820e-Abstract-Conference.html)

Stout and Goldie show how common random numbers synchronize stochastic events
across runs, create paired same-individual comparisons, reduce variance, and
support correlation-aware inference. They caution that unsupported event
synchronization can yield incorrect conclusions and that multiple paired
replications remain necessary.
[Keeping the Noise Down](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2761656/)

Buffalo, Pearson, and Klein formalize execution invariance for agent-based
counterfactuals. Their 2026 preprint shows with a toy infection example that a
shared seed with a stateful random-number generator can shift downstream noise
after control flow diverges; event-keyed counter-based generation restores a
stable event/noise mapping, while the cross-world event match remains a
modeling commitment.
[Realizing Common Random Numbers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.11084)

This project may adopt dependency tracking, simulation-time trace capture,
paired replication, and stable event identities. It proposes to establish
serial/parallel behavioral parity and causal lineage before using concurrency
to expand authority-architecture comparison.

## 9.7 What the bounded corpus leaves open

The full combination of persistent engine-authoritative social lives,
registered authority allocation, serialized persistent ancestors with stable
event/noise identities, provenance-bearing epistemic episodes, direct
trajectory and observer dead-end outcomes, and high-throughput paired
architecture comparison was **not located within the bounded corpus reviewed
for this charter**. This is a scoped research hypothesis, not a field-wide
priority claim.

The reviewed corpus also leaves material adjacent literatures outside its
boundary, including metareasoning and adjustable autonomy; causal and digital
twin simulation; wider common-random-number and variance-reduction methods;
event sourcing, epistemic provenance, and belief revision; planning livelock,
dialogue degeneration, and observer evaluation; and reproducibility in
parallel/distributed discrete-event simulation. Those areas require continued
review before stronger contribution language would be supportable.
